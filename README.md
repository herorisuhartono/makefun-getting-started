make.fun hosts software competitions called bounties. Each bounty describes a task, a deadline, entry requirements, and a scoring method. People build entries with AI coding agents and submit them for review and scoring.

This guide follows the usual entry path, from account setup through claiming a reward. Start with [my make.fun referral link](https://make.fun/r/6thkj69k); the referral code is `6thkj69k`. You can revisit [the same signup link](https://make.fun/r/6thkj69k) when you are ready to create an account.

## How bounties work

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| Open | Choose a live bounty and read its task, deadline, and rules. |
| Submit | Send the required repository or source bundle and the required work record. |
| Check | Automated checks confirm the entry follows the bounty's submission rules. |
| Score | The published scoring process evaluates eligible entries. |
| Pay | Entrants who earn a reward can claim it under the bounty's payout rules. |

The bounty page is the source of truth for its entry fee, schedule, rubric, and reward breakdown. Some bounties are free to enter; check the page before starting. Rewards for make.fun bounties are funded in advance and held in escrow, then eligible winners claim their allocated payout.

## What you need

- An AI coding agent and its session history, since bounties can require evidence of how the work was produced.
- A GitHub account for a public repository, unless the bounty accepts another format.
- Node.js 22 for the make.fun kit's command-line tools.
- A Solana wallet for a USDC reward claim.

## 1. Create your account

Visit [https://make.fun/r/6thkj69k](https://make.fun/r/6thkj69k) and choose an offered sign-in method. Then link the GitHub account you will use for entries and connect a Solana wallet for claims. Confirm both links in your profile before moving on.

## 2. Pick a bounty

Browse the bounty list, then read a candidate's full task, rules, scoring rubric, reward schedule, and close time. Check whether it requires a particular tool, deliverable, or entry fee. Only begin after you know what must be submitted and how it will be judged.

## 3. Get the kit

Open the bounty's kit link from its page and follow the included setup instructions. A kit can contain the rules, sample data, starter files, and command-line utilities. Read its rules before changing a starter; use the bounty's own instructions for local checks and submission options.

## 4. Build your entry

Create the entry in the format the bounty asks for, usually in a public repository with an open-source license. Keep your agent's work in a session that the kit can record, and make sure the final files match the submission requirements. Run any local grader or checks provided by that specific kit.

## 5. Submit it

Submit the repository or source bundle, the exact commit or version being entered, and any transcript or supporting files the bounty requests. Follow the bounty page or kit instructions exactly. Save the resulting entry details so you can check its status.

## 6. Check it, fix it, resubmit

Use the kit's status command or the entry page to see whether the submission passed its checks. If it was rejected, read the reported reasons, correct the issue, and submit again before the deadline. Check the bounty's resubmission limit and make sure the intended version is the one entered.

## 7. Claim your reward

If your entry earns a reward, follow the claim instructions shown for that bounty. Payouts are in USDC on Solana, and bounty funds are escrowed before the competition starts. Check the claim details and destination wallet carefully before confirming a transaction.

## Let your agent handle the workflow

An agent can read the bounty, inspect the kit, build the project, run checks, and prepare a submission. The make.fun kit is designed to work with common coding agents and harnesses; availability depends on the local tools and session logs you use. Give your agent the bounty rules and ask it to explain each proposed submission step. You remain responsible for account access, public repository settings, and the final submission.

## Earn with your own referral link

After creating an account, find your referral link and code in your profile. Share the link with people who may want to try make.fun. Referral terms and any rewards are determined by the current referral program; check its page for details rather than assuming a signup guarantees earnings.

## FAQ

### Does signing up cost anything?

The competition described here has no entry fee. Other bounties may differ, so check their pages.

### Is a reward guaranteed if I submit?

No. Submission is not a promise of placement or payment; results depend on the bounty's rules and scoring.

### Do I need to use one particular AI agent?

Use an agent and harness that the kit can work with and that can provide any required session record. Confirm compatibility in that bounty's kit.

### Can I submit without GitHub?

Some bounties may accept a source bundle, but formats vary. Follow the specific bounty's instructions.

### What should I do if checks fail?

Read the failure details, fix the named issue, and resubmit if the bounty permits it and its deadline has not passed.

### How do I avoid missing the deadline?

Check the bounty page's close time in your local time zone, and leave time for checks and a possible resubmission. A submission started before closing may still need to finish before the stated deadline.

### Where does a payout go?

Rewards are claimed in USDC on Solana to the wallet specified in the claim flow. Verify that wallet before confirming.
